# Structural Review: PRD — Enterprise GitOps Platform (HPDC)

**Reviewer:** Structural Editor (bmad-editorial-review-structure)
**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Source:** `prd.md` — 765 lines, ~8,318 words
**Audience:** Platform engineers, backend developers, SOC analysts, business stakeholders
**Structure model:** Pyramid (recommendation-oriented, top-down)

---

## Executive Summary

The PRD is well-organized with clear section hierarchy and consistent FR formatting. The primary structural issues are: **(1)** the Vision section front-loads implementation detail instead of intent, **(2)** the Glossary contains duplicate entries and verbose definitions that bloat the document by ~20%, and **(3)** inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags create a maintenance burden against the Assumptions Index. Total estimated reducible words: **~2,850** (34% of document).

---

## Prioritized Recommendations

### 1. CUT — §0 Document Purpose + "Generated by" line
**Rationale:** The Document Purpose section (lines 15–21) restates information already present in the frontmatter meta (title, audience, status) and the opening tagline. The "Generated by" line is metadata noise for the target audience — platform engineers and SOC analysts don't need to know which model produced the document. The purpose is implicit in the document title and opening paragraph. Remove the entire §0 section and the "Generated by" line from the meta block.
**Word impact:** −120 words

### 2. MOVE — §1 Vision, v1 proof-of-concept note to opening
**Rationale:** The v1 scope note ("v1 is a proof-of-concept...") sits at the bottom of §1 (line 35), buried after 280 words of dense architecture. This is the single most important framing decision in the document — it tells every reader how to calibrate expectations. Move it to the first sentence of the Vision section, before any architecture description. Pyramid principle: lead with the conclusion.
**Word impact:** 0 (relocation)

### 3. CONDENSE — §1 Vision from 3 paragraphs to 1
**Rationale:** The Vision section is 280 words across 3 paragraphs that attempt to describe: (a) the platform purpose, (b) the reference architecture and tech stack, (c) the GitOps delivery pipeline, and (d) the scaling model. This is an architecture summary masquerading as a vision statement. Condense to ~120 words: one paragraph stating what the platform does, who it's for, and why it exists. Move the tech stack enumeration and GitOps pipeline detail to §4 feature descriptions where they belong.
**Word impact:** −160 words

### 4. CUT — §3 Glossary, duplicate entries
**Rationale:** Two pairs of exact duplicates exist:
- **"Pulsar"** appears at line 113 and line 121 (identical text)
- **"Pulsar Functions"** appears at line 114 and line 122 (identical text)

These are copy-paste artifacts. Remove the second occurrence of each.
**Word impact:** −120 words

### 5. CONDENSE — §3 Glossary, verbose definitions
**Rationale:** Many glossary entries are 2–3 sentences when a single clause suffices for the target audience. Example: "Casbin" (line 87) is 38 words — "Authorization library implementing RBAC, ReBAC (Google Zanzibar), and ABAC models" is the useful part; "Runs as gRPC external authorization filter at Envoy Gateway" belongs in §4.10 FR descriptions. Apply this trim pattern across all entries. Target: ≤20 words per entry.
**Word impact:** −600 words

### 6. CUT — §3 Glossary, entries that belong in Features
**Rationale:** Several glossary entries are really feature descriptions disguised as vocabulary:
- "Cross-region visibility" (line 97) — this is FR-35's description
- "Device Communication Microservice" (line 98) — this is FR-11's dependency
- "Hub-and-Spoke" (line 107) — this is an architecture pattern, not a glossary term; belongs in §1 or §4.8

Move these to their respective feature sections or cut them. A glossary defines terms; it doesn't describe system behavior.
**Word impact:** −100 words

### 7. CONDENSE — §4.x Feature description blocks
**Rationale:** Each feature section opens with a "Description" paragraph (§4.1 through §4.11) that ranges from 80–150 words. These descriptions repeat information already stated in the FR titles and their consequences. For example, §4.1's description (lines 141–142) restates FR-1 through FR-4 in prose form. Condense each description to 1–2 sentences: what this capability does and why it matters. Let the FRs carry the detail.
**Word impact:** −700 words

### 8. PRESERVE — §4 FR structure (Consequences + testable criteria)
**Rationale:** The FR format — title, description, testable consequences, out-of-scope callouts — is excellent. It's scannable, testable, and unambiguous. This is the highest-value-density section of the document. Do not change the FR structure.
**Word impact:** 0

### 11. QUESTION — §8 Open Questions, mixed decision types
**Rationale:** The 15 open questions mix three distinct categories:
- **Strategic decisions** (Q1 payload format, Q6 Pulsar/Kafka split, Q9 SPA framework) — need stakeholder input
- **Implementation details** (Q2 ClickHouse DDL, Q3 KeyDB topology, Q4 CouchDB cluster size, Q5 YugabyteDB RF, Q7 Harbor backend, Q8 Spin language, Q12 Talos version) — these are engineering decisions, not PRD open questions
- **Missing requirements** (Q10 Backup/DR, Q11 Resource sizing, Q13 Region configs, Q14 Canary thresholds, Q15 Casbin policy schema) — these are gaps that should be resolved before implementation

Split into three subsections: **Decisions Needed**, **Implementation Decisions** (defer to architecture), **Requirements Gaps** (must resolve before sprint 1).
**Word impact:** 0 (restructuring)

The §9 index becomes the single source of truth. Reference it with a one-time note at the top of §4: "Assumptions are indexed in §9."
**Word impact:** −250 words

## Top 3 Findings

1. **Glossary bloat (§3):** Duplicate entries (Pulsar, Pulsar Functions) and verbose definitions account for ~820 words of waste. The glossary should be a quick-reference lookup, not a feature description repository. Trimming to ≤20 words per entry and removing duplicates saves ~20% of total document length.

2. **Vision front-loads implementation, not intent (§1):** The 280-word Vision section buries the v1 scope note (the most important framing decision) at the end and spends 200+ words listing technology names. Platform engineers scanning the document need to know "what is this and what ships first" within 10 seconds. The current structure requires reading to line 35 before that's clear.

3. **Inline [ASSUMPTION] tags create dual maintenance (§4 + §9):** Every assumption exists in two places — inline in FR descriptions and in the §9 index. This violates the single-source-of-truth principle and guarantees drift. Removing inline tags and keeping §9 as the authoritative index saves ~250 words and eliminates a maintenance risk.
