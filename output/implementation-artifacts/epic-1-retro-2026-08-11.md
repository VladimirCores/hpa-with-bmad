## Successes

1. **Deepest story records in the roadmap.** All six files run 110+ lines with Dev Agent Records, requirements, source-tree components, testing standards, anti-patterns, and completion notes — the quality bar set on the first epic.
2. **The no-fork pattern was born here.** 1-5 owns the Cilium mTLS manifest and epics.md explicitly says "do not fork a second mTLS manifest under Epic 3" (revalidated by 3.9). 1-6 owns Harbor and 2-1 explicitly reuses it ("implemented as Story 1.6, reused for Epic 2 Story 2.1"). The reuse contract is documented, not accidental.
3. **Substrate discipline.** Version pins throughout (Talos 1.13.7, Cilium 1.19.6, Rook-Ceph 1.20.3, Harbor 2.11.3), persistent QEMU disks that survive reruns, state only in Ceph, destructive OSD init gated behind explicit cleanup flags.

## Challenges & Growth Areas

1. **Plan drift in the cleanest epic.** 1-6 is delivered and tracked but never added to epics.md's Epic 1 story list (ends at 1.5). The plan, tracker, and records disagree on epic size.
2. **No baseline commits.** Zero baseline commits recorded across 1-1..1-6 despite full-depth records — the audit trail is not pinned to delivery commits.
3. **Cross-system drift lineage.** Harbor's home is Epic 1 (1-6), its consumer is Epic 2 (2-1), and the config facts live in `gitops/harbor/base/harbor-values.yaml` plus installer scripts — the reuse model this epic established is the ancestor of the 10-2 harbor drift guard.

## Key Insights

1. The reuse model (one manifest, multiple epics) is powerful but concentrates drift: the more epics share one artifact, the more parity guards matter.
2. Record depth (110+ lines) and record pinning (baseline commits) are independent qualities — Epic 1 has the first and not the second.
3. An unplanned but legitimate delivery (1-6) should be reconciled into the plan, not silently left as a plan/tracker mismatch.
