## Successes

1. **Architecture boundary enforcement.** Each data type maps to its owning store by design (documents→CouchDB, graph→ArcadeDB, state→YugabyteDB) with cross-store joins via Hasura in 6-4.
2. **Executable fidelity.** CRUD/change-feed/graphql stories shipped real Python CLIs with tests (`entity-api.py`, `entity-change-processor.py`, `graphql-gateway.py`) — a fidelity step up from pure-declarative stories, without leaving the offline constraint.
3. **Quantified contracts.** 200ms p99 mutation, 500ms reaction, bulk limit 1000, exactly-once via Restate, DLQ routing.

## Challenges & Growth Areas

1. **Offline validation ceiling.** The Python CLIs prove contract shape, not that production services hit the budgets (Hasura 2s join, Restate exactly-once, 200ms p99) — live-cluster + soak required (P0-008/P0-023 class).
2. **Cross-epic route coupling.** The `/gql` route created here was the subject of Epic 10's shadowing fix — topology ownership needs documenting.

## Key Insights

1. Real executable CLIs + tests are the highest-fidelity offline proof available; they validate contract shape, not runtime.
2. Quantified budgets recur across epics — a single cross-epic SLO-verification register is warranted.
3. Route-topology ownership (`/gql` → entity-store) must be explicit to prevent cross-epic shadowing regressions.
